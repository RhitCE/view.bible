Map of the Route of the Exodus

The Route of the Exodus · Exodus 12 to Numbers 33: the forty-two stages of the journey from Rameses to the plains of Moab

The Route of the Exodus, version 1. Sources: the New King James Version, the Copernicus GLO-30 relief, OpenStreetMap’s rivers, OpenBible.info’s places and Ussher’s Annals as a check. Click the map to enlarge.Full size

A relief map of the forty-two stages of the journey in Numbers 33, from Rameses in Egypt to the plains of Moab across from Jericho. Moses wrote the stages down at the command of the LORD (Numbers 33:2); the map joins them with straight lines in the order Scripture gives, from the crossing of the sea to Mount Sinai, by Kadesh, and around Edom.

Each site follows OpenBible.info’s first identification of the place named in the verse, with its score of confidence out of 1,000; a score under 700 is marked ‡, as disputed, and thirty-five of the forty mapped stages fall below that line. Mount Sinai is set at Jebel Musa; its other proposed sites are drawn faded. The thirty-eight years of wandering are shown as places only, most known only from this list, not joined by the route line.

The relief comes from the Copernicus GLO-30 elevation model and the towns are placed by the OpenBible.info Bible Geocoding data; where a site is uncertain, the map follows its best estimate. Names follow the New King James Version.

Map by View.Bible. Relief from the Copernicus GLO-30 elevation model (ESA, 2021); rivers and streams from OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL); site positions from OpenBible.info (CC BY 4.0).

1446–1406 BC: 1 Kings 6:1 puts the Exodus 480 years before Solomon’s fourth year, 966 BC, the Kings chart’s reckoning; Ussher’s reckoning gives 1491–1451 BC. Both are reckonings from the years and ages Scripture gives, not statements of Scripture (how the years are counted).

Mount Sinai

Mount Sinai is set at Jebel Musa, OpenBible.info’s first identification for the place (a score of 465), with the Wilderness of Sinai, the twelfth stage. Its other proposed sites are drawn faded on the map: Har Karkom (103), Jebel Sin Bishar (89) and Jebel Serbal (54).

The stages not mapped

The seventh stage, by the Red Sea (Numbers 33:10–11), names no place and is not mapped. Rissah, the eighteenth stage, has only one proposed site, Sharma, which lies in Arabia, south of this map, and so is not shown either.

The thirty-eight years

The stages of the thirty-eight years (Numbers 33:18–36, Rithmah to Ezion Geber) are shown as places only, not joined by the route line: most are known only from this list, and OpenBible.info puts several at one site (Kehelathah and Makheloth; Mount Shepher and Haradah; Tahath, Terah and Mithkah, beside Kadesh; and Moseroth with Mount Hor).

How sites are marked

Each site is OpenBible.info’s first identification of the place named in the verse, followed to the modern site; the score is its confidence out of 1,000. A site scored under 700 is marked ‡, as disputed: thirty-five of the forty mapped stages fall below that line.

The crossing of the sea

Exodus 14:2 sets the camp before Pi Hahiroth, between Migdol and the sea, opposite Baal Zephon; OpenBible.info puts Pi Hahiroth and Baal Zephon both at Tell Defenneh. Where the sea was crossed is not known, and the line from Pi Hahiroth to Marah marks no crossing place.

Around Edom

Numbers 20:14–21 and 21:4 say that Edom refused Israel the way through its land, so the journey went around it. OpenBible.info’s sites for Zalmonah, Punon and Oboth lie in and beside the Arabah, so the line on the map crosses it twice; the sites set that course, not the text.

Other places on the map

Ten more places appear on the map besides the forty-two stages. Migdol and Baal Zephon (33:7; Exodus 14:2) lie by the crossing of the sea. Arad (33:40; Numbers 21:1–3) and Hormah (Numbers 14:45; 21:3), and Heshbon (Numbers 21:25–30) and Jahaz (Numbers 21:23), lie near the route east of the Jordan. Jericho (33:48, 50) and the Abel Acacia Grove (33:49) lie across the Jordan from the plains of Moab, and Mount Nebo (Deuteronomy 32:49; 34:1–5) is where Moses viewed the land.

The Salt Sea

The Salt Sea is drawn over its southern basin, as in the days of Moses and Joshua: all the ground lower than 385 m below sea level. The base map’s level for about 2000 BC, 415 m below sea level, is not used here.

The natural cover

The ground cover shown on the map, modelled for about 2000 BC.

Natural cover: the potential natural vegetation under today’s rainfall (1981–2020), taken as the setting c. 2000 BC, when the land stood near a woodland high point with a drought setting in (Langgut et al. 2015; Kaniewski et al. 2018; Palmisano et al. 2019). Modelled by the rules of Danin 1992 (Flora and vegetation of Israel and adjacent areas, Bocconea 3) on rainfall, height and rock, checked against Danin’s map, Soto-Berelov et al. 2012, Al-Eisawi 1985 (Jordan) and Abi-Saleh and Safi 1988 (Lebanon). Rainfall: the CHELSA V2.1 climatology 1981–2010 (Karger et al., CC0) corrected to rain gauges (GHCN, NOAA; national normals) for the rain shadow of the ridges.

Egypt’s waters and cover

The Delta’s lakes, shores and branches, and Egypt’s natural cover.

The Faiyum lake c. 2000 BC at its low stand, 20 m below sea level (Marks et al. 2018). The Bitter Lakes a salt flat (contested), Lake Timsah a marsh and a seasonal lake in the western Wadi Tumilat (Moshier and Hoffmeier 2015). The coast south-west of Tell el-Farama, before the Tineh plain formed (Stanley et al. 2008; Goodfriend and Stanley 1999); the Canopic promontory as marsh and lagoon (Flaux et al. 2017). The Ahramat Branch digitised from Ghoneim et al. 2024 (CC BY 4.0). Natural cover in Egypt: the Nile valley, Delta and Faiyum follow flood, water and ground, not rain (Butzer 1976; Marks et al. 2018). Sinai follows the vegetation types of Boulos 2008, its northern sands fixed (Karnieli and Tsoar 1995); the rain rules of Danin 1992 hold there and on the coast west of the Delta. Modern rainfall is kept: near-modern aridity had come by c. 2500 BC (Butzer 1976). Rainfall: CHELSA V2.1 (Karger et al., CC0) corrected to rain gauges (WMO normals and GHCN, NOAA; El-Bagouri et al. 1995).
